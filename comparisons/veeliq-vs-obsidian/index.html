VeelIQ vs Obsidian.

Obsidian is where many people think and write. VeelIQ is how their AI gets to use what they wrote. Less a rivalry than a division of labor.

Obsidian is a beloved local-first knowledge tool: Markdown files on your disk, bidirectional links, a graph view, and a vast plugin ecosystem. It is built for humans capturing and connecting ideas. VeelIQ Context is built for a different reader: the AI assistants you use every day. It hosts your documents and memories behind an MCP endpoint so any client can retrieve them semantically. The overlap ("my knowledge") invites the comparison; the purposes are almost orthogonal.

Strengths of Obsidian

  • Full ownership: plain Markdown files, offline, no vendor dependency.
  • Exceptional for human thinking: links, graph, daily notes, canvases.
  • Endlessly extensible through community plugins.

Strengths of VeelIQ

  • AI-native from the start: chunking, embeddings, and semantic retrieval need zero configuration.
  • Serves every MCP client at once — no per-app plugin wrangling.
  • Adds memory: your AI accumulates facts about you, which no notes app does.
  • Nothing to maintain: no vault sync, no plugin updates, no local indexing.

Limitations to know

  • VeelIQ is not a place to write or organize notes, and it is a hosted service — if strict local-only storage is a requirement, Obsidian's model fits better.
  • Obsidian requires assembly for AI use: plugins, API keys, and per-machine setup, with results that vary by plugin quality.

Better together

A common pattern: keep writing in Obsidian, and upload the notes that matter — project briefs, reference docs, decision logs — to VeelIQ so every AI client can retrieve them. Obsidian remains the thinking surface; VeelIQ becomes the retrieval surface. See how that looks in the private AI knowledge base use case.
